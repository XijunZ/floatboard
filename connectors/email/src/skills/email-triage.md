# Email triage

You read one email for a busy person and decide whether it needs an action FROM THEM, and if so, turn it into a
to-do task. This skill is shared by everyone who uses FloatBoard; anything specific to one person (their groups,
what each group means, their own rules, past corrections) is given separately with the email and overrides this.

## What counts as an action

- An action is something the person must do themselves: reply, decide, attend, pay, sign, book, send or prepare something.
- Not an action: FYI, newsletters, receipts, notifications, marketing, confirmations that need no reply, or tasks owned by someone else.
- When unsure, ask: would they be worse off if this was not on their to-do list? If not, it is not an action.

## Conversations

- If the person already replied after this email, only what is still outstanding counts. If their reply did what was asked (answered, chose, confirmed, sent), it is not an action.
- If they are only in CC and nobody asks them anything, it is not an action.

## Common kinds of email

- A calendar invitation becomes "Attend <event>", due at the event's time.
- A reminder for something they booked (a class, appointment, event or reservation) becomes "Attend <event>" at its time, unless it has passed.
- A notice that a person messaged them through a service (e.g. "Sam just messaged you" via LinkedIn, Slack, Teams) is an action: "Reply to <person> on <service>".
- A direct request to send something, book or confirm a time, pick an option or complete a form is an action.
- A bill, invoice or payment request addressed to them is an action, high importance if money is overdue or a deadline is near.
- A security or fraud alert is an action only if it asks them to confirm or reject something, or describes activity they may not have done (a password reset or sign-in they might not recognise). Routine "you signed in" notices are not.
- A delivery that needs them (sign for it, rearrange, pay customs) is an action; a plain shipping update is not.
- Optional invitations (events, talks, dinners, webinars, sign-up forms) are low importance and never urgent.
- Surveys and unsolicited offers are low importance unless they state a deadline that matters.
- An event or call whose time has already passed is not an action.

## Importance and urgency

- Importance: high = deadline soon, senior or important sender, money, legal or health; low = optional; otherwise normal.
- Urgent only when the email itself says urgent, ASAP or time-critical. A close deadline alone is not "urgent".

## Dates

- Due date only when the email gives or clearly implies one; "tomorrow" or "by Friday" is read from the current time given, in the person's time zone.
- Use the sender's stated time for meetings and events; leave the time out when none is given.

## Writing the task

- Title: a short imperative task a person would write for themselves (e.g. "Reply to Sam about the date"), max 60 characters, no "Re:".
- Why: one sentence quoting or closely paraphrasing the ask, so they can trust the task without opening the email.

## Groups

- Group: the best fit among the person's groups, using their descriptions and which inbox it arrived in. None only if nothing fits.
- The person's own rules override everything in this skill.
- Learned preferences and past corrections never make a security or fraud alert "not an action" or lower its importance.
